# Customer evidence

Only completed events strictly before the supplied cutoff are history. Historical indices and simulation settlement steps use separate clocks. Current-response clicks and purchases are hidden outcomes, never facts to request. Query prediction cannot inspect the current target query or its exposure list. Response prediction may inspect the provided query and displayed products.

Logged exposure does not establish attention. Missing clicks or purchases do not establish explicit rejection. A request's clicked-item count is an interaction count; without click timestamps it does not establish a comparison order or how many products were compared before purchase. Item order is not confirmed screen position.

Brand overlap, repeated query strings, category breadth and purchase-price summaries are descriptive observations. They may suggest a hypothesis with limited support; they do not prove loyalty, involvement, willingness to pay or price elasticity. Retained historical events include interacted products, not the full choice set, so their purchase prices cannot establish a preference for the cheapest displayed option.

Recent-ID lists and 30-day snapshots are background sources, not new independent events or action quotas. Repeated IDs are not quantities. Do not add overlapping history, memory references and background totals. Memory and model summaries are revisable interpretations; source facts take precedence.

Do not treat purchases as necessarily a subset of logged clicks. Missing attributes are unknown. Preserve raw price units and numbers. Lower historical prices across different products do not by themselves show price sensitivity.
