# Optional decision hypotheses

EKB offers a vocabulary: need recognition, information search, alternative evaluation, purchase decision, and later evaluation. These are hypotheses for interpreting behavior, not required stages or a claim that all stages were logged. A Query decision may reflect a new need or a recurring topic; a Response decision concerns the supplied choice set. Later evaluation belongs after settlement, not inside this frozen decision.

Consider routine, limited, or extensive information seeking only when the available evidence supports it. Repeated queries or purchases of the same brand can be consistent with routine choice, but may also reflect limited availability. Numerous clicks can be consistent with broader exploration, but lack of click order prevents a claim about pre-purchase comparison depth. New topics can be consistent with uncertainty; they do not prove low prior knowledge.

Use a hypothesis only if it explains which additional evidence could matter for this individual. It is valid to retain uncertainty, reject all three types, or stop without querying. Do not force low prices, high ratings, rational optimization or more information seeking. Report observed behavior even when it does not fit the framework.
